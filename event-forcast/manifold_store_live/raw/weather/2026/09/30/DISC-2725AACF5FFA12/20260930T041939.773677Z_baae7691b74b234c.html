Prognostic Discussion for Week 3-4 Temperature and Precipitation Outlooks
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD
300PM EDT Fri Sep 25 2026

Week 3-4 Forecast Discussion Valid Sat Oct 10 2026-Fri Oct 23 2026

As the autumn transition progresses, a combination of tropical and extratropical climate signals may influence the current Week 3-4 Outlooks. In the tropics, the strengthening El Niño is evident, marked by weekly sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies exceeding 2°C in the Niño 3.4 region. Correspondingly, enhanced convection across the Central Pacific—reflected in outgoing longwave radiation patterns—could initiate Rossby wave trains to the extratropics. Additionally, the Madden-Julian Oscillation remains heavily dominated by the ongoing El Niño signal. Beyond the tropics, other atmospheric drivers are also shaping extratropical circulation; notably, the North Pacific Oscillation (NPO) is projected to stay in its positive phase through the beginning of the forecast period.

Across North America, a wave-train pattern is indicated by the manual blend of dynamical and statistical tools. Forecasts show anomalous troughing positioned over both the Gulf of Alaska and northeastern Canada, alongside anomalous ridging over the central contiguous United States (CONUS) as well as the North Atlantic. The persistence of this structure in the recent Days 8–14 forecasts highlights some stability to this pattern. While this wave-train pattern does not project directly onto either El Niño or the NPO, it contains features consistent with both drivers and may reflect a combination of the two. Although all dynamical models support this general wave-train configuration, key discrepancies introduce uncertainty into the temperature forecast. Specifically, the GEFS depicts a broader region of positive height anomalies extending from the central CONUS to New England, whereas other dynamical models restrict these positive heights primarily to the central CONUS. Meanwhile, the JMA exhibits weak heights across the majority of the CONUS, excepting the Pacific Northwest.

Driven by this anomalous ridging over the CONUS, the Week 3-4 Temperature Outlook indicates a tilt toward above-normal temperatures for much of western CONUS, with the highest probabilities centered over the Great Basin and southern California. Enhanced by anomalously warm Gulf SSTs, above-normal temperature probabilities also extend eastward across Florida and the Gulf Coast. Equal Chances (EC) of above-, near-, or below-normal temperatures are forecast for the rest of the CONUS. Although a few forecast models hint at below-normal temperatures, this indication is mostly limited to Week 3, with no clear signal persisting into Week 4.

The Week 3-4 Precipitation Outlook continues to favor elevated chances of above-normal precipitation across the Desert Southwest, supported by weak troughing in the manual blend of tools. Although the Southwest monsoon typically winds down by September, tropical moisture can still linger into late season. Additionally, an active East Pacific hurricane season increases the probability of post-tropical moisture moving into the region. Conversely, ahead of the anomalous ridging, a subtle lean toward below-normal precipitation is forecast for the Northeast.

Across Alaska, dynamical models show mixed agreement on the precise position of anomalous troughing; however, they show broad anomalous onshore flow along the Panhandle and South Coast. Consequently, the forecast favors above-normal temperatures in eastern Alaska, alongside above-normal precipitation across the South Coast and Panhandle.

Under ongoing El Niño conditions, temperatures in Hawaii are favored to be above normal. Precipitation guidance is more mixed, though a slight tilt toward a wet signal is indicated for Maui, Oahu, and the Big Island. Although precipitation is typically below normal during El Niño events as the wet season approaches, the active hurricane season could foster increased precipitation across the islands.


Forecaster: Laura Ciasto

The shading on the precipitation map depicts the most favored category, either above-median (A), below-median (B), or near-median with the solid lines giving the probability ( >33%) of this more likely category (above, below, or near).

In areas where the likelihoods of 2-week mean temperatures and accumulated precipitation amounts are similar to climatological probabilities, equal chances (EC) is indicated.
