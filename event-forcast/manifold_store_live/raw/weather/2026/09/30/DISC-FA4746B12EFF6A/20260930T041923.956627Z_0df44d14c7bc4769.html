+ Short Range Forecast Discussion (Day ½-2½)
Short Range Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
337 PM EDT Tue Sep 29 2026

Valid 00Z Wed Sep 30 2026 - 00Z Fri Oct 02 2026

...Considerable flash flooding remains possible tonight from southeast
Arizona and New Mexico into West Texas as the remnants of Polo merge with
a strong upper-level low...

...Heavy rain and flash flooding shift to the southern Rockies and
Southern Plains on Wednesday and Thursday, with a broad area of flood
concerns extending to the Great Lakes...

..Warm, dry weather builds across the East behind the departing
Nor'easter, with highs approaching 90 from the Mid-Atlantic to the Ohio
Valley by Thursday...

An upper-level low over the Desert Southwest is forecast to move into New
Mexico by Wednesday morning, drawing tropical moisture from the remnants
of Hurricane Polo northeastward. Slow-moving thunderstorms will continue
this evening across southeast Arizona and western New Mexico, with a line
of heavy storms expected overnight from southern and eastern New Mexico
into West Texas, including the Pecos River valley. Saturated soils, burn
scars, canyons, and low-water crossings are especially vulnerable, where
locally significant flash flood concerns exist. WPC has a Moderate Risk
(level 3 of 4) of excessive rainfall from the Southwest into the central
High Plains, including a targeted area along the Arizona and Utah border.
Storms near the remnant circulation could also produce damaging winds and
a brief tornado over southeast New Mexico and West Texas.

On Wednesday, a dry slot should move across eastern New Mexico and the
Panhandles, finally ending the heavy rain over those hard-hit areas.
Meanwhile, a strengthening low over the High Plains will pull deep Gulf
moisture northward. Moderate Risks are in place for upslope rain along the
southern Front range and for a slow-moving line of storms from central
Oklahoma into Texas. A Moderate Risk also extends across southeastern
Nebraska into western Iowa. SPC has a Slight Risk (level 2 of 5) risk of
severe thunderstorms over the Southern Plains, with damaging winds, hail,
and a few tornadoes possible. By Thursday, the trough should weaken and
stall over the Plains as a cold front settles south. The Moderate Risk
then shifts to southeast Oklahoma, the Texas Hill Country, and South
Texas, with a Slight Risk from Texas to the Great Lakes. Cooler, drier air
will follow the front, and frost is possible across the northern Plains
and Upper Midwest late in the week.

In the East, the nor'easter will move away from New England, ending the
rain. Minor coastal flooding remains possible at high tide along parts of
the Mid-Atlantic coast. High pressure will bring a warming trend, with
highs 10 to 15 degrees above average. Heat indices near 110 are possible
over South Texas on Wednesday before clouds and rain arrive. In the West,
a ridge will build, bringing dry weather to the Northwest and above
average temperatures to California and the Great Basin. Offshore winds
will keep fire weather concerns elevated over parts of California.


Eovino

+ Medium Range Forecast Discussion (Day 3-7)
Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
354 PM EDT Tue Sep 29 2026

Valid 12Z Fri Oct 02 2026 - 12Z Tue Oct 06 2026


...Major Multi-day South-Central to East-Central U.S. Excessive
Rainfall/Flooding threat this week with tropical connections...


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

The latest 00/06/12Z guidance remains fairly agreeable on the
overall synoptic pattern through the period. An initial upper-wave
departing the East Coast will leave a trailing frontal boundary
meandering through the Southeast to south-central U.S. before
eventually progressing south as another upstream upper-
wave/trailing cold front approaches from the northwest. Guidance
agrees this upper-wave will amplify over the eastern U.S. and drive
the surface fronts further south towards the Gulf by mid-next
week. Meanwhile, another upper-wave upstream will bring another
frontal system along the northern tier towards the north-central
U.S. with good guidance agreement and continuity for this late into
the period. The greatest uncertainty during the period looks to
remain related to the progression of both frontal boundaries which
will impact the location/coverage of the anticipated heavy rainfall
through the period. The 13Z NBM initially provides a good
compromise between the deterministic/AI/mean guidance with the QPF
footprint across the southern Plains to Lower Miss/Tenn Valleys
Friday/Saturday. However, thereafter, the GEFS mean remains more
aggressive particularly with the northern extent of QPF coverage
from the Tennessee Valley/Appalachians and into the Mid-Atlantic
which leads to higher totals further north in the NBM compared to
the rest of the guidance. Ultimately favored a compromise between
the NBM and ECens mean across these locations late weekend into
early next week. The rest of the updated WPC forecast package used
a composite blend of the best clustered deterministic/AI guidance
(GFS/ECMWF/UKMET/ECMWF AIFS/AI GFS) through the first half of the
period. An initially small contribution from the GEFS/ECens mean is
ramped up to 40% of the blend by day 7/Tuesday given some
increasing deterministic spread, though all guidance continues to
remain fairly well clustered overall given the lead time.


...Pattern Overview and Weather/Hazards Highlights...

A closed upper trough/low with wavy frontal systems will shift
slowly eastward over the South-central U.S. later week in southern
stream split flow. Widespread excessive rainfall/flooding threats
over the South-central U.S. will slowly shift across the East-
central/Southern U.S. as further focused by Gulf/Southeast Coast
frontal genesis. This event will be fueled by a tropical moisture
connection from current East Pacific Hurricane Polo which will
weaken as it tracks through Northwest Mexico. There will also
develop an increasingly deep and protracted lead Gulf inflow
pattern with tropical influence. This expansive multi-day threat
should prove slow to progress late week wedged between upper ridges
over the Southwest and Southeast U.S.. Activity focus will then
slowly work downstream into the East. Expansive WPC Excessive
Rainfall Outlook Marginal Risk areas with embedded Slight Risk
areas cover the Southern Plains through the Tennessee Valley and
Central Gulf Coast states then into the Appalachians and vicinity
for the Day4/Friday and Day5/Saturday periods.

Meanwhile, systems within a more progressive/zonal mean northern
stream flow will support passage of a series of wavy frontal
systems across the U.S. northern tier of the nation from the
Northwest through the Northeast this week. These will act to
provide a northern rainfall shield focus and over-running for
lifting moisture/clouds and deliver moderating post-frontal fall
temperatures to spread/sink southward with high pressure settling.
This scenario will also act to focus/confine a lead heavy rainfall
main threat axis to The South and the Southeast early next week.
Activity will progress slowly from the East-central U.S. to the
East later period with the greatest confidence in additional heavy
rainfall shifting from the Southern Plains/Lower Mississippi Valley
into the Tennessee Valley/southern Appalachians/southern Mid-
Atlantic and then eventually to the southeast Atlantic/Gulf Coasts
with southward frontal progression. These EROs and supporting WPC
Hazards Outlook areas are all linked below.

Upper-ridging will lead to a period of above-average temperatures
over the western U.S. including highs into the 90s and low 100s for
central/southern California and the Desert Southwest. Some heat
may reach hazardous levels for southern California this
weekend/early next week.


Putnam/Schichtel

Hawaii Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
510 AM EDT Tue Sep 29 2026

Valid 00Z Wed 30 Sep 2026 - 00Z Wed 07 Oct 2026

...Nolo is expected to bring heavy rainfall to portions of the
eastern islands midweek...

Hurricane Nolo is forecast to continue its northward track, west
of the islands until early Wednesday when it is expected to stall
about 250 nm west of Kauai before turning and continuing west
through the remainder of the period. The outer rainfall bands east
of the center are likely to produce locally heavy accumulations
across Kauai and Niihau through Thursday and may possibly produce
areas of flooding and mudslides, especially in areas of steep
terrain. Refer to the advisories/statements from the National
Hurricane Center for the latest information regarding Hurricane
Nolo.

Southeasterly flow in the wake of the storm will support above-
normal PWs into the latter part of the week before winds weaken
and then turn more northeasterly, bringing drier across the
islands by the weekend.

Spread across the model suites remains significant, however both
the GFS and ECMWF indicate that an upper low closing off well
northeast of the islands may be sufficient to push a front into
the islands early next week.

Pereira

NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
855 PM EDT Tue Sep 29 2026
Day 1
Valid 01Z Wed Sep 30 2026 - 12Z Wed Sep 30 2026

...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM THE
SOUTHWEST INTO THE CENTRAL HIGH PLAINS...

01Z Update...
The threat of flash flooding...with some localized considerable
impacts...will continue throughout the overnight hours from the
Southwester U.S. across the southern portions of the Rockies and
out onto the adjacent central Plains. Radar and satellite trends
from the late afternoon and early evening allowed for some of the
outlook areas to be trimmed from the western extent.
However...moisture with a subtropical connection remains in place
with favorable upper flow pattern to maintain the threat. As the
moisture emerges over the plains and interacts with an amplifying
synoptic scale trough over the central portion of the
country...shifted the axis of excessive rainfall threat southward
to follow the trend first started in the 29/12Z suite of guidance,
Otherwise...the changes to the on-going outlook were generally
small.

Introduced a Marginal Risk area over the southwestern Florida
peninsula as an area of shower and thunderstorms approached the
coastline as of 00Z along an inverted trough.

Bann

16Z Update: The main premise of the forecast remains unchanged with
a significant, widespread heavy rainfall threat across much of
AZ/NM into portions of west TX as the tropical remnants of Polo
make their way northward, contributing to a prominent flash flood
prospect over the Desert Southwest into the Southern High Plains.
12z CAMs continue to pinpoint a significant heavy rain threat
across southeastern AZ, including the Tuscon area where 2"/6-hr
probabilities running >70% for much of the area, a threshold that
typically verifies more substantial flash flood prospects in that
area, historically. The environment across the Southwest remains
ripe for heavy rainfall rates between 1-2"/hr and potential for
widespread 2-4" totals, locally higher given the deep moisture
profiles stemming from advection of Polo's moisture being pulled
poleward. Latest 500mb analysis indicates the evolution we have
been anticipating the last several forecast cycles as the mid-level
energy from Hurricane Polo begins to interact and couple with the
upper level low (ULL) currently positioned over the Colorado River
basin out between AZ/CA/NV. Expectation is for the main energy from
Polo to become fully incorporated into the ULL leading to the
addition of stronger vorticity advection into the overall setup
that will materialize over the course of the D1 and beyond.

Time frames of note will include now through 00z across southeast
AZ as the initial surge of convection will occur over the area with
a quasi-linear band of heavy rainfall will initiate over eastern
Pima county and slide east-northeast through the course of the
afternoon. This will lead to widespread flash flood concerns over
the I-19 corridor from the border to north of Tuscon with the
complex of thunderstorms likely expanding east into impact areas
along and north of I-10 into western NM. This area is where the
signal is very prevalent for rainfall totals >3" with the latest
12z HREF blended mean QPF between 3-5" over the area just northeast
of Tuscon into western NM with the maximum located within the
Apache and Gila National Forests. Latest probabilities for >3" are
substantial in that same area with the extension of 60-90+%
probabilities all the way into Tuscon proper which enhances the
threat of the locally significant flash flood concerns in the
region.

As we expand further east, the rest of NM will become inundated
with widespread heavy rainfall late-afternoon through the overnight
time frame with the heaviest rainfall positioned over the southern
and eastern area of the state. Remnant moisture and vorticity
advection being absorbed into the upper pattern will co-locate
within the area from far west TX to points northeast leading to
substantial convective coverage, as well as some organization
trends that are conducive for more appreciable heavy rain
footprints. The areas of concern will lie within the Upper Trans
Pecos from Clines Corner down to the southeastern reaches of NM,
including the more flash flood prone areas along the Pecos river
(Roswell-Artesia-Carlsbad) where the combination of small stream
and arroyo flooding and urbanization will likely provide an avenue
for more locally significant flash flood prospects this evening as
a prolific line of thunderstorms moves through the area. Latest 12z
KEPZ RAOB this morning came in with PWATs of 1.55", a daily
maximum already established for the date, and well beyond the 95th
percentile for seasonal climatology. Current forecast is for PWATs
to rise further, putting the region within historical thresholds
when it comes to some of the higher PWAT readings in general. This
type of environment typically precludes more substantial flash
flood concerns, and this setup is no different. Expect the Eastern
NM High Plains, Upper Trans Pecos, Sacramento Mountains, and
adjacent West TX High Plains from the TX Panhandle into the Permian
Basin to be under the threat of enhanced flash flood chances
through the back half of D1, bleeding into D2.

A targeted MDT was added over the AZ/UT for the central portions of
the bordering area due to another round of convection occurring
this afternoon under the influence of the ULL. Ongoing flooding
over the area enhances sensitivities for the area with local FFG's
basically as low as they could go before more flooding ensues. In
coordination with the local SLC and FGZ WFO's, a MDT risk was added
to target this specific area with an additional 1-2" of precipitation
possible in any convection that occurs this afternoon. This threat
will wane with the loss of diurnal heating around sunset.

Broad SLGT risk continues over the Central Plains with a bit more
of an isentropic pattern materializing across KS into NE/IA which
will likely include some convective schemes, but less instability
and a less robust deep moisture presence compared to areas of the
Southwest will limit the threat to more scattered flash flood
concerns this evening. 12z HREF EAS probabilities for >1" are still
fairly robust (50-80%) over the area from nort-central KS up
through the Missouri River Valley and adjacent Corn Belt areas of
IA, but the >2" signal drops off considerably which notes a limit
to the upper threshold of the forecast which curbs the threat
closer to a SLGT for this period.

Kleebauer

Day 2
Valid 12Z Wed Sep 30 2026 - 12Z Thu Oct 01 2026

...THERE ARE MODERATE RISKS OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS
OF THE CENTRAL AND SOUTHERN PLAINS, AS WELL AS THE CORN BELT...

...Front Range to Southern Plains...

20Z Update: Maturing lee cyclone across the Central and Southern
High Plains will allow for a continued heavy rain threat across the
Sangre de Cristos into the San Juan Range as the flow shifts out
of the northeast along the northwest periphery of the surface low,
creating a textbook upslope flow regime to perpetuate the rainfall
over the terrain of southern CO into northern NM. The prospects of
flash flooding will be enhanced due to the antecedent moist soils
brought about by prior periods of rainfall, some of which will
likely cause flash flooding in their own right, leading to a
heightened probability for flooding given the overlapping nature of
heavy precip. 12z HREF continues to advocate for significant
totals, 2-4" with locally higher, of additional precip within the
mountains of the Southern Rockies into the Front Range as the
combination of upslope flow and enhanced axis of deformation will
lead to two primary periods of heavy precip; once in the morning
and then once again in the evening as we see the surface low
intensify and strong mid- level vorticity advection pivots around
the ULL center. HREF probabilities, both EAS and neighborhood
probabilities for >2" remain very high across the aforementioned
area leading to high confidence of the heavy rain threat with
multi-day totals likely breaching 3" over a large area over the
Front Range of southeastern CO into northern NM. The previous MDT
risk was maintained with little adjustment given the continued
signal.

Across the Southern Plains from Oklahoma down through Texas, the
setup is more classic with regards to increasing low-level
convergence along a propagating cold front that will materialize
from the surface cyclogenesis over the High Plains. The flow will
be fairly meridional ahead of the slow-moving mean trough/ULL
moving through the Southwest U.S. creating a broad area of
relatively backed flow across portions of west TX into the Red
River Valley down through the Central Rio Grande Valley (RGV) and
Edwards Plateau by Wednesday evening into early Thursday morning.
Model consensus is for the initiation of heavy convection along the
front as it migrates out of the High Plains of TX with the western
periphery of precip likely developing from the western Rolling
Plains down into the Lower Trans Pecos and adjacent Concho Valley
by late Wednesday afternoon. This will set the stage for a
broadening convective footprint over the Southern Plains as the
warm sector environment with deep tropical moisture presence will
interact with the frontal progression as it slowly moves eastward
through the area Wednesday night. Models indicate a formidable
35-45kt LLJ structure situated from the RGV up into the Central
Plains as the warm sector maturation will be well-defined with
continued moisture advection poleward until the front passes over
any given area. MLCAPE between 1500-2500 J/kg bisecting the eastern
two-thirds of the region will offer plenty of instability to
couple with 30-40kt Effective Bulk Shear, creating an environment
conducive for stronger mesocyclone cores and merging multi-cell
clusters that will provide heavy rain for a large area come
Wednesday evening.

HREF neighborhood probs for >3" are upwards of 60-90% over the
area from the Lower Trans Pecos to the central Red River Valley
between TX/OK with an average of 40-60% over the entire stretch
from Eagle Pass, TX up towards Norman, OK. This is a substantial
area of heightened probs for 3" with modest probs (25-50%) for >5"
locally with the best signal over the Lower Trans Pecos and
northern Hill Country between San Angelo and Fort Worth. This is
more than sufficient for a continuation of the MDT risk with some
adjustment to account for a bit slow forward propagation forecast
on the northern edge of the line that should keep the worst of the
rainfall just west of the DFW metroplex. This will be an area to
keep watching over the next 24 hrs as the threat for more
appreciable urban flooding in a scenario where the line makes
headway into the metro.

...Central Plains into the Corn Belt...

The maturing cyclone over the High Plains will lead to a
progressive warm front on the eastern flank of the low with a
general warm sector evolution occurring from the Southern Plains
into the southern reaches of the Midwest. Rainfall from isentropic
ascent will already be occurring on the northern periphery of the
low, but limited instability will maintain more "subdued" rates
that are coincident with more appreciable flash flood prospects.
By Wednesday evening, that will no longer be the case as the warm
front progression will open the door to a more favorable
environment with modest instability development along and south of
the front which is classic for these maturing lee cyclones. PWAT
anomalies of +2 to +3 deviations will have advected into the region

...THERE ARE SLIGHT RISKS OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS OF
SOUTHERN TEXAS ON DAY 4 AND FROM THE CENTRAL GULF COAST INTO THE
TENNESSEE VALLEY ON DAY 5...

2030Z Update...
Deterministic and ensemble guidance maintained decent run to run
continuity that resulted in few changes needed to the previously
issued Excessive Rainfall Outlook on either Day 4 or Day 5. The
large scale picture still features deep moisture streaming
northward from the Gulf of American into place ahead of a large
scale mid-latitude trough digging south- and east-ward, Spaghetti
plots from the EC ensembles suggested expanding the outlook areas
westward a bit...moreso on Day 4. But the change does not reflect a
fundamental shift in forecast reasoning.

Bann

Previous Outlook Discussion...

Day 4...
Mid-level energy associated with the trough moving into
the central and southern Plains on Thursday will remain over the
region into at least the early part of the weekend. Deep moisture
will remain as well over parts of the southern Plains --
maintained by backing low level winds tapping Gulf moisture and
drawing it westward into a lingering low level boundary. As this
moisture interacts with the slow-moving mid-level energy that is
forecast to remain over the area, there will continue to be some
potential for heavy rainfall producing storms, impacting some areas
that are likely to be left vulnerable to excessive runoff by the
previous day(s) heavy rains. While forecast confidence has
diminished some, there remained enough of a signal in the
overnight guidance that showed additional heavy accumulations
centered across parts of South to South-Cental Texas to maintain a
Slight Risk over the region. Farther north and east, a cold front
will continue to settle southeast through the Ohio Valley, with
some potential for heavy rain along and ahead of the boundary.
Supported by deep southerly flow along the western extent of the
subtropical ridge, guidance continues to show a broadening pool of
anomalous moisture spreading from the Gulf Coast into this
boundary. Mid-level energy embedded within the flow may help
produce areas of heavy rain, with some guidance indicating heavy
amounts along the Gulf Coast from Mississippi to the Florida
Panhandle this period.

Day 5...
Moisture will continue to deepen from the Gulf Coast northward into
the Tennessee Valley -- with PW anomalies increasing to 2-3 std
dev above normal across much of the region during this period. A
Slight Risk was drawn along the axis of deepest moisture (PWs at
or above 2.25 inches) which is expected to extend from the central
Gulf Coast northward into an area of enhanced ascent centered in
the vicinity of the Tennessee Valley. The previously noted front
dropping south into the area along with mid-level energy emanating
from the Plains are expected to help focus heavy rainfall
development over this region. Forecast confidence was limited in
part by model spread regarding the placement of the front, which is
likely to play a role in where the heaviest amounts occur.
Therefore, notable adjustments to at least the northern extent of
the Slight Risk may be required in future updates.

Pereira

...Major Multi-day South-Central to East-Central U.S. Excessive
Rainfall/Flooding threat this week with tropical connections...


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

The latest 00/06/12Z guidance remains fairly agreeable on the
overall synoptic pattern through the period. An initial upper-wave
departing the East Coast will leave a trailing frontal boundary
meandering through the Southeast to south-central U.S. before
eventually progressing south as another upstream upper-
wave/trailing cold front approaches from the northwest. Guidance
agrees this upper-wave will amplify over the eastern U.S. and drive
the surface fronts further south towards the Gulf by mid-next
week. Meanwhile, another upper-wave upstream will bring another
frontal system along the northern tier towards the north-central
U.S. with good guidance agreement and continuity for this late into
the period. The greatest uncertainty during the period looks to
remain related to the progression of both frontal boundaries which
will impact the location/coverage of the anticipated heavy rainfall
through the period. The 13Z NBM initially provides a good
compromise between the deterministic/AI/mean guidance with the QPF
footprint across the southern Plains to Lower Miss/Tenn Valleys
Friday/Saturday. However, thereafter, the GEFS mean remains more
aggressive particularly with the northern extent of QPF coverage
from the Tennessee Valley/Appalachians and into the Mid-Atlantic
which leads to higher totals further north in the NBM compared to
the rest of the guidance. Ultimately favored a compromise between
the NBM and ECens mean across these locations late weekend into
early next week. The rest of the updated WPC forecast package used
a composite blend of the best clustered deterministic/AI guidance
(GFS/ECMWF/UKMET/ECMWF AIFS/AI GFS) through the first half of the
period. An initially small contribution from the GEFS/ECens mean is
ramped up to 40% of the blend by day 7/Tuesday given some
increasing deterministic spread, though all guidance continues to
remain fairly well clustered overall given the lead time.


...Pattern Overview and Weather/Hazards Highlights...

A closed upper trough/low with wavy frontal systems will shift
slowly eastward over the South-central U.S. later week in southern
stream split flow. Widespread excessive rainfall/flooding threats
over the South-central U.S. will slowly shift across the East-
central/Southern U.S. as further focused by Gulf/Southeast Coast
frontal genesis. This event will be fueled by a tropical moisture
connection from current East Pacific Hurricane Polo which will
weaken as it tracks through Northwest Mexico. There will also
develop an increasingly deep and protracted lead Gulf inflow
pattern with tropical influence. This expansive multi-day threat
should prove slow to progress late week wedged between upper ridges
over the Southwest and Southeast U.S.. Activity focus will then
slowly work downstream into the East. Expansive WPC Excessive
Rainfall Outlook Marginal Risk areas with embedded Slight Risk
areas cover the Southern Plains through the Tennessee Valley and
Central Gulf Coast states then into the Appalachians and vicinity
for the Day4/Friday and Day5/Saturday periods.

Meanwhile, systems within a more progressive/zonal mean northern
stream flow will support passage of a series of wavy frontal
systems across the U.S. northern tier of the nation from the
Northwest through the Northeast this week. These will act to
provide a northern rainfall shield focus and over-running for
lifting moisture/clouds and deliver moderating post-frontal fall
temperatures to spread/sink southward with high pressure settling.
This scenario will also act to focus/confine a lead heavy rainfall
main threat axis to The South and the Southeast early next week.
Activity will progress slowly from the East-central U.S. to the
East later period with the greatest confidence in additional heavy
rainfall shifting from the Southern Plains/Lower Mississippi Valley
into the Tennessee Valley/southern Appalachians/southern Mid-
Atlantic and then eventually to the southeast Atlantic/Gulf Coasts
with southward frontal progression. These EROs and supporting WPC
Hazards Outlook areas are all linked below.

Upper-ridging will lead to a period of above-average temperatures
over the western U.S. including highs into the 90s and low 100s for
central/southern California and the Desert Southwest. Some heat
may reach hazardous levels for southern California this
weekend/early next week.


Putnam/Schichtel

Forecaster's Toolbox (Prototype)
 
Tools Generated at WPC
These tools are NOT operationally supported. Data may not update regularly due to workstation failure and/or data unavailability.
Experimental Urban Rain Rate Dashboard (URRD)

Visualizes the probability of high rain rates that may lead to flash flooding over the next several days at over 60 major U.S. cities.

Prototype Specialized Excessive Rainfall Maps

Interface for specialized WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook Maps for NWS County Warning Areas and States.

Local Storm Reports

Custom plots of Local Storm Reports across the Contiguous United States. Reports include rain, snow, ice, and severe weather, as well as other significant information from storm spotters.
Extreme Precipitation Monitor

Displays the climatological significance of precipitation forecast by WPC. The climatological significance is represented by Average Recurrence Intervals (ARIs) of precipitation estimates from NOAA Atlas-14 and Atlas2.
Ensemble Situational Awareness Table

An interactive situational awareness table that displays anomalies, percentiles, and return intervals from the GEFS, NAEFS, and ECMWF Ensembles (login required to view ECMWF data).

*Please note that there is currently an issue where only users on a NOAA network can access this page. We are actively working to resolve this problem.

NDFD Forecast Temperature Records

Interactive display of where temperatures could approach or exceed records within the contiguous U.S. (based on NDFD temperature forecasts)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    

NWS NDFD Max/Min Temperatures and Departure from Normal

Displays Days 1-7 NDFD maximum and minimum temperatures, along with their respective departures from climatology.

Precipitation Object Tracking

Contains 4 different tools to track precipitation objects (heavy rain and snowfall) from short-range high-resolution deterministic and ensemble guidance.

Weather in Context Prototype

Displays forecast information and its climatological context to quickly alert a forecaster when a record or neear-record breaking event is possible. This tool is available for both CONUS and Alaska.

Automated Forecast Low Clusters

Displays 0-72 hour cyclone forecast positions from global ensemble and deterministic model guidance.

Intense Rainfall and Flash Flood Reports

Displays flood and flash flood reports as well as intense rainfall observations for user-selectable time ranges and customizable geographic regions. Includes ability to download reports and associated metadata in csv format.

Experimental HeatRisk

The experimental National Weather Service (NWS) HeatRisk is a color-numeric-based index that provides a forecast of the potential level of risk for heat-related impacts to occur over a 24-hour period, with forecasts available out through 7 days.

GEFS Probabilities

Plots of GEFS probabilistic forecast of precipitation, temperature, and sea-level pressure exceeding various thresholds.
1/3/6/24-hr Changes

Change in weather parameters (temperature, dewpoint, surface pressure, etc) over the last 1/3/6/24 hours. Data is provided from the Real-Time Mesoscale Analysis (RTMA) or the Rapid Refresh (RAP).
Other Favorite Forecast Tools
Analog-Based Probability Guidance

Analog guidance that uses an objective approach to find historical events that are similar to the upcoming forecast.
National Blend of Models

Nationally consistent and skillful suite of calibrated forecast guidance based on a blend of both NWS and non-NWS numerical weather prediction model data and post-processed model guidance.
Atmospheric River Portal

A portal for atmospheric river forecasts and diagnostics from the Center for Western Weather and Water Extremes.
GEFS Plumes

An interactive display of time series plots from GEFS ensemble members at a point.
SPC Forecast Tools

A variety of useful tools generated at The Storm Prediction Center (SPC).
ECMWF Forecast Charts

Output from the ECMWF Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), including medium range deterministic, ensemble, and machine learning model forecasts.
